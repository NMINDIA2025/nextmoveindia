How to Improve English Speaking at Home: 10 Daily Activities for Kids

Practical English-speaking activities for children that parents can use at home, plus when a live class can add guided practice.

NextMove India Parent Guide · Practical ideas for home and live learning

Many parents want their child to speak English more comfortably, but daily practice does not need to feel like another school subject. A few short, repeatable activities can create more opportunities to listen, think and speak.

1. Start with a five-minute English routine

Choose one predictable time each day. Ask simple questions such as “What did you enjoy today?” or “What do you want to do tomorrow?” The goal is regular speaking, not perfect grammar.

2. Use picture talk

Show your child a picture and ask them to describe what they can see. Younger children can name objects and actions. Older children can explain what may have happened before or after the picture.

3. Retell a short story

After a story, ask your child to retell it in their own words. Encourage sequence words such as first, then, next and finally.

4. Turn everyday tasks into conversation

Cooking, shopping, packing a school bag or getting ready for bed can become speaking opportunities. Ask your child to explain what they are doing instead of correcting every sentence.

5. Practise useful sentence starters

Give children flexible phrases such as “I think…”, “In my opinion…”, “I would like to…”, and “My favourite part was…”. These help them start speaking when they do not know what to say next.

6. Let mistakes be part of practice

If every sentence is interrupted for correction, children may become hesitant. Model the correct version naturally and keep the conversation moving.

7. Add reading aloud

Short, age-appropriate reading aloud can support pronunciation, rhythm and confidence. Keep sessions brief and choose material the child actually enjoys.

8. Record one minute of speaking

Once or twice a week, let your child speak for one minute on a familiar topic. Comparing recordings over time can make progress easier to notice.

9. Practise questions, not only answers

Conversation is two-way. Ask your child to interview you, then encourage follow-up questions. This builds listening and spontaneous speaking.

10. Give practice a clear purpose

Instead of saying “Speak in English”, give a small challenge: explain a game, describe a favourite character, tell a joke or present three facts about an animal.

When should parents consider a live class?

Home practice is valuable, but some children benefit from a regular teacher, peer interaction and structured speaking opportunities. A live class can provide a consistent environment for guided conversation, phonics or public speaking practice.
